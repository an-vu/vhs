vHuman, Year Five

Home improvement.

The only thing that’s changed is everything. The new vHuman website brings software, interactive experiences, and 3D work into one clear visual system, designed around the person exploring it. Typography, generous spacing, and restrained interaction keep the interface simple, so the work in front of you is always the focus.

The site is built as lightweight static pages, so it loads fast and stays simple to maintain. Interactive galleries, motion, and real-time 3D appear where a project earns them, and a great deal of time went into what was left out. Design and engineering are treated as one system, and the site is built the way the studio builds everything else.

Reality Check

Spatial, computed.

Image coming soon

Reality Check is a performance diagnostic tool for 3D assets headed into augmented reality and spatial computing. Each USDZ model is examined across geometry, materials, textures, object count, and file size to identify where complexity is concentrated and where bottlenecks may appear.

File size alone does not explain how a model will perform. Reality Check extracts measurable features from each asset and uses Python and scikit-learn to analyze their relationship with observed performance, building a more complete picture of the demands a model places on an interactive environment.

The analysis shows where performance pressure is most likely to come from, giving designers and developers a clear starting point for optimization before an asset reaches the final experience.

Kobachi

A little space for everything.

Image coming soon

Kobachi is a modular personal dashboard that brings projects, links, media, personal information, and everyday tools into one place. A card-based layout gives each kind of content its own space while keeping the whole page easy to scan and organize.

Each card takes on a role suited to what it holds. A project, photograph, link collection, or interactive tool gets a layout fit for its purpose while the page keeps its structure, so nothing is forced into the same format.

Content and layout are kept separate, so the dashboard grows without a redesign for every addition. Kobachi combines enough structure to keep information clear with enough flexibility to make each space feel personal.

Studio Kit 26

Made to measure.

Image coming soon

Studio Kit 26 is the latest version of vHuman’s in-house toolset, designed around the recurring needs of daily production work. This release introduces Atlas, Spare Time Dynamics, Flow, and Spatial Capture, along with updates across the existing lineup. The same kit is available to the partners and clients we work with, so a project runs on one pipeline end to end.

Atlas turns any place on Earth into an explorable 3D environment. Terrain, roads, buildings, parks, and water come directly from mapped sources, so every scene begins with the actual structure of a place rather than a procedural approximation.

In supported regions, Atlas can also take a city through time. Historical records anchor what was built and when, while employment patterns, planned projects, and infrastructure drive a demand-based model of how the city could grow over the decades ahead. Past and future live in the same environment, so a city can be explored across time as easily as across space.

Spare Time Dynamics turns complex 3D source assets into clean, editable models. Each one arrives organized the way it was built, with its parts in place and working rather than flattened into a single mesh. Exports that took hours of manual cleanup are ready in minutes, and the pipeline adapts to each asset’s structure instead of forcing everything through one generic import path. The finished model keeps all the detail of the original while becoming far easier to customize, animate, and reuse.

Flow is the system behind every gallery on this site, built for presenting large image collections without making the presentation itself the focus. Images sit on angled cards in an endless loop, so a collection of any size stays easy to move through, scan, and return to. Everything adapts to any screen, and the interface around the imagery stays minimal.

Spatial Capture brings real-time LiDAR scanning and testing into the studio’s workflow. Depth data appears as it’s collected, making it practical to evaluate real-world geometry on the spot and prototype interactive 3D experiences against it.

Every existing plugin, add-on, and utility moves forward with new features and support for current software. The work gets more ambitious every year, and each release sharpens the tools behind it, keeping everything fast to develop, test, organize, and present.

Iris

Look. That’s enough.

Image coming soon

Iris is a digital reader built on eye-tracking technology developed at Brown University. A standard laptop webcam is all the hardware required to turn eye movement into an input method for opening books, navigating pages, and controlling the reading experience without a mouse or touchscreen.

Accurately tracking the eyes is only one part of making that interaction useful. Calibration, smoothing, dwell timing, visual feedback, and interface design come together to decide when looking should become an action and when it should simply remain part of reading, forming a more controlled and intuitive layer between the technology and the person using it.

Iris gives the research a focused everyday use. Eye control offers another way to interact when hands are occupied, speaking is inconvenient, or conventional controls are difficult to use. By placing the technology inside something as familiar as reading, an ordinary webcam becomes an alternative input device.

Wind Tunnel

Light, speed.

Image coming soon

From 2022 to 2024, vHuman took high-detail 3D source models, a race car and five aircraft, and rebuilt them to run in real time across rendering, AR, VR, and interactive software. Each project worked the same problem from a different angle, and each one left the pipeline better than it found it, turning one-off optimization into the studio’s standard process for preparing heavy assets without losing the detail that made them worth building.

Rocky (2022)

The first vHuman project was a detailed recreation of the Red Bull RB18, created with a 3D artist and reworked so a render-grade asset could perform in real time. Geometry, materials, and scene structure were restructured for performance, with a series of rendered studies produced alongside the technical work.

Lightning II (2022)

An intricate F-35 model was rebuilt around real-time use and maintained as one version that works across visualization, AR, and VR rather than a separate build for every destination. The project turned the approach from Rocky into a repeatable workflow while keeping the character of the original model intact.

Tomcat (2023)

The Tomcat was the first articulated model through the pipeline. Variable-sweep wings, layered components, and moving surfaces shaped how mechanical assets are organized, animated, and kept light.

Raptor (2023)

Built for a government security client, the Raptor focused on efficiency without visible loss. Materials and textures were consolidated, draw-heavy complexity was reduced, and a level-of-detail workflow was built so a single asset scales from phone to headset.

Spirit (2023)

As a flying wing, the Spirit is one continuous surface where every flaw in topology and shading shows. The model required cleaner geometry and physically consistent materials, and introduced automated audits of geometry and asset weight before export, moving optimization from manual passes toward a defined pipeline.

Raider (2024)

The Raider applied everything the program had developed as a single disciplined process covering geometry, materials, hierarchy, export, and real-time performance, and closed the program with its cleanest and most portable asset.

Scarecrow AI

Finally, a brain.

Image coming soon

Scarecrow AI explores how image recognition can help with the repetitive work of identifying and labeling corn imagery. The model suggests whether a plant looks healthy or unhealthy, and people review the results and correct what it gets wrong.

Those corrections feed back into training through an active-learning loop, so the model improves with every review session instead of treating its first prediction as final.

By letting people watch their own feedback change the model’s accuracy, Scarecrow AI makes machine learning something a farmer can see working rather than something they are asked to believe in.

Pit Wall

Race the data.

Image coming soon

Pit Wall is a motorsport strategy simulator built around real drivers and their past race data, played from the strategist’s seat. Driver performance, fuel use, tire wear, and pit decisions come together in a changing race model, where a call made on one lap can shape what happens several laps later.

Originally built around NASCAR, the system was later adapted for Formula 1 and other racing formats. Configurable race rules and reusable driver and vehicle profiles let the simulation accommodate different series, using historical performance as the starting point.

A live dashboard follows the race as it unfolds, its wraparound perspective angling information toward the viewer like a curved, spatial display. Every lap advanced, pit stop called, and reset requested runs through the same interface underneath, so a race can be driven by hand or by script.
